\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Large language models answer regulatory questions fluently while citing the wrong
provision, quoting a rule that omits its own exception, or presenting an
unsupported conclusion. In aviation such failures are consequential: an answer
must be traceable to authoritative, versioned legal evidence. This project studies
citation-grounded question answering over Regulation~(EU)~No~376/2014 on
occurrence reporting, reframing an initial comparison of few-shot prompting,
retrieval-augmented generation and parameter-efficient fine-tuning into a
failure-analysis question: where does retrieval-grounded question answering break
down, and which condition observable at runtime justifies escalating instead of
answering?

The evaluation instrument was validated before its measurements were believed.
Four aviation professionals independently reviewed a bounded sample of the
benchmark; they agreed on the verdict for fewer than half of the records
(Fleiss' $\kappa$ = 0.250), which is reported as a finding rather than resolved
away. The reviewed benchmark of 98 evidence-linked questions
was then frozen under a pre-registered rule.

Retrieval is not where the system fails: hybrid retrieval with cross-encoder
reranking places acceptable evidence in the top five results for 89.3\% of
held-out questions. The failure is citation precision. Under an evaluator that resolves references
against the corpus rather than matching strings, the retrieval-augmented baseline
identifies the correct article for 62.5\% of questions but reproduces the complete
citation, sub-paragraph included, for 44.3\%.

Two results shape the resulting architecture. Deterministic quote verification
and evidence-selection errors did not co-occur once, so both checks are required.
And across 140 generated answers the model never once used its explicit,
cost-free abstention channel --- including on answers that fabricated a source
identifier. Abstention cannot be delegated to the generator; it must be imposed
from outside, on evidence the system can check. The escalation policy is
specified accordingly, with its retrieval-uncertainty threshold left explicitly
uncalibrated and the sample size that calibration would require stated.
